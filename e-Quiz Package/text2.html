Fire Safety Training in Workplaces

[A] Appropriate training in fire safety, which usually includes briefing sessions and fire drills, should be arranged to all people working in the premises at least once a year.
[B] In workplaces where the public will be present, training should be given to every employee preferably twice a year.
[C] Managers should ensure staff who work in shifts or outside normal hours are included in such training.
[D] If an employee's knowledge of Chinese or English is limited, the fire safety training should be given in a manner that they can understand.
[E] Written instructions should be provided in a language that the staff can read.

Adapted from 'Guidance Notes in Fire Safety at Workplaces' published by the Labour Department

🎯 Language Focus

1. Relative Clause

Usage

A relative clause can give information to the object(s) that it immediately follows. It begins with a relative pronoun to describe different things.

A relative clause is not a complete idea and cannot standalone. It must be connected to a main clause.

Feature

We can identify a relative clause and its object with the relative pronouns below:

Relative pronoun who which that where
Object(s) to describe people thing(s) people or thing(s) at a place

Example

Example 1:

Written instructions should be provided in a language that the staff can read.

↑ Main Clause      ↑ Relative Clause

Example 2:

Appropriate training in fire safety, which usually includes briefing sessions and fire drills, should be arranged to all people working in the premises at least once a year.

↑ Main Clause    ↑ Relative Clause    ↑ Main Clause (continues)

2. Identifying Important and Additional Information

There are two types of relative clauses: defining and non-defining.

  • Defining relative clauses give important information about someone or something that helps define the object of the clause.
  • Non-defining relative clauses give additional information about a person or thing. It is extra information.

Feature

When reading a complex sentence with a relative clause, we can differentiate important and additional information with commas (,).

Type of Information Use of Comma (,)
Important No commas around defining relative clauses
Additional Comma(s) is/are used before/around non-defining relative clauses

Example

1. Important information

In workplaces where the public will be present, training should be given to every employee preferably twice a year.

⚠️ No commas - this information defines which workplaces

2. Additional information

Appropriate training in fire safety, which usually includes briefing sessions and fire drills, should be arranged to all people working in the premises at least once a year.

ℹ️ Commas used - this is extra information about the training

💡 Language Tip: Looking for the Word Family

We can group related words together into a word family. By adding prefixes and suffixes to a root word, we can form a word family to help us recognise and remember the meanings of the words.

Example:

  • 🌏 Japan → Japanese
  • 👥 Employ → Employee, Employer
  • ⚠️ Danger → Dangerous, Dangerously, Endanger

How to find word families:

  1. Visit any online dictionary
  2. Enter a word (e.g., "danger")
  3. Check out its word family in the dictionary entry
